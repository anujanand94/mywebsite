8. Concept Unlocked

Recurring Revenue Quality
- What it is (plain English): Not all subscription revenue is created equal. $1 of subscription from a customer who chose to subscribe and uses the product weekly is worth multiples of $1 from a customer who got auto-enrolled and forgot to cancel.
- The mechanism: Forced or low-intent subscribers churn faster, complain louder, and trigger regulatory risk. Markets eventually discount the headline ARR by a "quality discount" once disclosure improves — and the stock re-rates.
- Today's live example: The Uber One CBC story is a real-time recurring revenue quality red flag. If Uber were forced to disclose active engaged Uber One members vs. total enrolled, the gap would be the short thesis.
- When to use this: Any subscription business growing ARR/MAUs faster than usage metrics (orders, GTV, session time) — that gap IS the quality problem.

Sentiment Washout
- What it is (plain English): The market moment when forced selling exhausts itself and the marginal seller is gone — typically marked by capitulation in the most-hated names.
- The mechanism: Short interest peaks, positioning surveys hit max bear, and any catalyst (today: Iran deal) triggers a violent short cover that overshoots.
