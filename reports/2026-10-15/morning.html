5. Concept Unlocked

FII Flows & Indian Market Mechanics- What it is (plain English): Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are overseas funds that move large sums in and out of Indian equities. Because they trade in dollars, their flows are tightly coupled to the dollar's direction, not just India's fundamentals. - The mechanism: When the dollar weakens, the currency math improves for a foreign investor — the same rupee gain is worth more dollars, and hedging costs fall — so capital flows into India; a strong dollar reverses it. This is why NIFTY can rally on a day the US tape is red. - Today's live example: DXY is -0.47% at 102.063 and USD/INR -0.24% at 95.055 (a stronger rupee) — and NIFTY 50 closed +1.66% while SENSEX jumped +2.11%, decoupling cleanly from the -1.28% Nasdaq futures. The softer dollar did the work the US selloff couldn't undo. - When this is your edge: When US and Indian tapes diverge, check the dollar first — a soft DXY is the tell that FII flows, not local news, are driving NIFTY.

Term premium- What it is (plain English): The extra yield a lender demands for locking money up in a long bond rather than rolling short ones — compensation for inflation, deficit-supply and duration risk over the holding period. - The mechanism: When that risk compensation falls, long yields drop even if the Fed path is unchanged — which is exactly how you tell a term-premium move from a Fed-path move: watch which end of the curve does the work. - Today's live example: The 30-year fell -3.8bp to 5.525% while the 3-month rose +2.8bp to 4.085% — the front end pricing hike risk, the back end paying down term premium simultaneously. - When this is your edge: When you need to know whether falling long yields signal easier Fed policy or just cheaper duration risk — mortgages and REITs key off the latter.
