1. Why This Industry Exists

Gold miners dig a metal that pays no interest, rusts never, and that humans have treated as money for 5,000 years. They exist because investors want insurance against currency debasement, inflation, and panic — but owning bullion earns nothing. Miners turn a flat price into leveraged profit. Portfolios hold them as crisis insurance with upside.

3. How the Money Works

Revenue = ounces sold × gold price. Miners are price-takers; they control only volume and cost. The one number that matters is AISC — all-in sustaining cost per ounce. Margin is simply (gold price − AISC). Agnico's all-in-sustaining costs were $1,459 per ounce in the second quarter, up roughly 14% year-over-year. Scale helps modestly, but orebody grade matters more — a rich deposit beats a big balance sheet. Great businesses sit low on the cost curve with long-life, high-grade mines. Think of it like a toll booth where you can't set the toll, only cut your own overhead.

4. The 4 Macro Drivers

Driver 1: Real Interest Rates

Mechanism: Gold pays no yield, so when real (inflation-adjusted) rates rise, the opportunity cost of holding it climbs and bullion falls — dragging miner revenue. Now: Post-Oct 28 Fed decision is the pivot; higher rates raise the cost of holding gold, which pays no interest. 2nd-order: Juniors watch the headline cut; the real move is in the real yield — nominal cuts amid sticky inflation are bullish; cuts that kill inflation expectations are bearish. Driver 2: Gold Price Level vs Cost Curve

Mechanism: Margin is spread, not price. Costs are sticky; the spread swings violently with gold. Now:Spot gold has retreated roughly $711 per ounce from its Q1 2026 record of $4,873, while industry AISC holds firm at $1,785 per ounce.

Driver 3: Energy & Input Costs (Oil)

Mechanism: Diesel runs the trucks and processing; oil is 15–20% of AISC. Now: Oil feeds in twice. Higher oil reaches the miners twice: through their costs, and through the rate channel, which weighs on gold. 2nd-order: Royalties, not fuel, are the quiet killer now. A 16% year-over-year jump in industry AISC was driven primarily by higher royalties, which scale with revenue and unwind only partially on the downside. Driver 4: US Dollar

Mechanism: Gold is priced in dollars; a stronger dollar makes it costlier globally, dampening demand and price. Now:The USD Index is moving higher after bottoming at its previous 2026 highs, with previous resistance turning into support.
2nd-order: Costs are often local-currency; a stronger dollar lowers AISC for miners operating in Canada, Australia, Africa — partially offsetting lower revenue. The FX hedge is natural. Threshold: DXY above 108 signals sustained headwind for bullion and multiples.

7. How to Value These Companies

Use P/NAV (net asset value, discounting each mine's cash flows) and EV/EBITDA — not P/E, because depreciation on huge capex distorts earnings. P/NAV ranges 0.7–1.3×; royalties trade 1.5–2.5×. EV/EBITDA runs 3.5–8× as we saw. The common junior mistake: anchoring on P/E and calling a 6× stock "cheap," ignoring that a falling gold price gutts next year's EBITDA through operating leverage. Always stress-test NAV at a lower gold deck.

9. Risk Map

Risk 1: Margin Squeeze from Sticky Costs

Gold falls but AISC — especially royalties and labor — won't follow fast, so margins collapse non-linearly. Transmission: a 15% gold drop can cut margin 25–30%, crushing EBITDA and the multiple on it. Gold mining stocks absorbed a 23% margin hit as spot gold dropped $711/oz from record highs while AISC held at $1,785. Precedent: 2012–2015 bust wiped out high-cost producers. Early warning: Q4 cost guides rising while realized price slips — exactly today's signal.

Risk 2: Jurisdictional / Political Shock

A government hikes royalties, nationalizes, or suspends permits. Transmission: ounces vanish from NAV overnight, forcing writedowns and multiple de-rating as risk premium spikes. Precedent: Barrick's Mali Loulo-Gounkoto dispute and historic Tanzania export bans stranded billions. Early warning: election cycles in host countries, rising "disputed" tonnage in filings, sudden tax-code chatter. This is why Agnico's safe-jurisdiction premium exists and why Barrick trades at half its multiple despite similar scale.

Risk 3: Capital Misallocation at the Top

Record cash tempts managers to overpay for M&A or chase low-grade growth. Transmission: value-destroying deals at cycle peaks inflate costs and dilute NAV, then get written off in the downturn. Precedent: the 2011 peak M&A binge (Barrick's Equinox, Kinross's Red Back) produced ~$30B in later impairments. Early warning: premium-priced acquisitions announced near gold highs, reserve "growth" coming via price assumptions not drilling. Discipline — buybacks over empire-building — separates survivors.

Risk 4: Grade Decline & Reserve Depletion

Miners dig their best ore first; grades fall over time, lifting unit costs structurally. Transmission: more rock processed per ounce means rising AISC regardless of gold price, a permanent margin headwind. Agnico Eagle saw less production in the first half of 2026 due to lower grades and throughput across certain mines. Precedent: sector-wide grade decline since 2000 doubled real costs. Early warning: falling head grades, flat production despite higher mining rates.

13. Three Questions You Should Be Able to Answer

Q1: Why can a miner report record earnings and still fall hard the next day?
A: Because the market prices the forward margin, not the printed quarter. Newmont posted record adjusted earnings and kept guidance, and shares still fell 4.6 percent, because the market looked at the fourth quarter: costs up, gold down. Operating leverage means the spread between gold and AISC drives the stock — and when Q4 guides show gold falling into sticky costs, a 27% margin cut is already visible. Yesterday's profit is a sunk fact; tomorrow's spread is the trade.

Q2: Why does oil hurt gold miners twice?
A: First directly — diesel is 15–20% of AISC, so pricier fuel lifts costs per ounce. Second through the macro channel: higher rates raise the cost of holding gold, and higher oil reaches the miners through the rate channel, which weighs on gold. So oil raises costs and lowers revenue simultaneously — a double hit to the spread. Juniors model only the fuel line and miss the rate transmission that compresses the gold price itself.

Q3: Bull vs bear case given today's macro?
A: Bull: miners trade at 5.7X earnings, 22% below their 20-year average, with record cash and a Fed easing into gold's favor. Bear: Newmont's Q4 AISC near $1,900 with gold near $4,000 shrinks margins ~27% — costs are winning. What flips it: a sustained gold floor above $4,200 restores the spread and forces the valuation re-rate.
