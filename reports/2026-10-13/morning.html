5. Concept Unlocked

Earnings revision cycle- What it is (plain English): The multi-quarter trend in whether analysts are raising or cutting their estimates for a company or sector. Prices follow the direction of revisions more than the absolute level of earnings. - The mechanism: Estimates move in herds — one cut triggers more cuts as analysts anchor to each other, so a revision is rarely a one-off; it's the start of a cycle that runs for quarters. - Today's live example: JPMorgan and Wells Fargo both missed, yet XLF is +0.74% — the market is betting these misses are the trough, with a steep curve (10y–3M +1.19%) about to push estimate revisions higher. If the next prints confirm that, the sector re-rates off a low bar. - When this is your edge: Buy when revisions are bottoming and sentiment is washed out — the inflection, not the absolute number, is where the money is.

PEG ratio- What it is (plain English): Price-to-earnings divided by the growth rate — a way to judge whether a high multiple is justified by how fast earnings are growing. - The mechanism: A P/E of 40 is cheap if earnings grow 50% and expensive if they grow 10%; PEG normalises the multiple against growth so you can compare fast and slow compounders. - Today's live example: AMD's premium multiple only holds if GPU earnings keep compounding fast — the moment a memory-cost spike threatens that growth rate (AMD -2.28% today), the PEG blows out and the "justified" multiple isn't anymore. - When this is your edge: Use it to spot when a high-flyer's growth is decelerating faster than its multiple is falling — that gap is where expensive names break.
