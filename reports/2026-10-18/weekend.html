Weekend Sector Deep-Dive

1. Why This Industry Exists

Every byte you stream, every AI prompt, every phone call needs a physical home: a building full of racks or a steel tower with antennas. These REITs own that physical layer and rent it out on long contracts. Tenants — cloud giants, carriers, enterprises — can't easily move, so the rent keeps coming. Portfolios hold them for inflation-linked, contracted cash flow tied to unstoppable data growth.

2. What's Happening Right Now

What happened: Two very different stories inside one "industry." Data centers ran hot on AI demand — Equinix Q2 revenue rose 16.4% YoY to $2.625B, beating consensus, with monthly recurring revenue up double digits for a third straight quarter.

Digital Realty's backlog hit a record $1.4 billion, with its development pipeline 63% pre-leased at an expected stabilized yield of 11.5%. Yet EQIX (-3.5% 1m) and DLR (-3.4%) fell, while towers AMT (+7.6%) and CCI (+12.6%) ripped. The group still beat SPY by 3.43pts over 1m and 2.92pts over 3m, but lags badly over 6m (-15.42pts).

Why: Rotation. Data centers are priced for perfection (EQIX forward multiple rich); capital is chasing the laggard towers, which had cratered on rate fear and churn. AMT traded near its 52-week low at $162.20 in early October — a value snap-back.

What it sets up: EQIX (Oct 28) and DLR (Oct 29) earnings will test whether AI leasing can re-rate the expensive names.

3. How the Money Works

Revenue is rent on long leases with built-in escalators — like a landlord who raises rent 3% automatically every year. Data centers add high-margin interconnection fees (tenants paying to plug into each other). The two costs that decide profitability: power (passed through, but availability is the real constraint) and interest (these are capital-hungry, debt-funded businesses). Scale helps — denser interconnection and cheaper capital. Equinix runs IBX colocation centers where enterprises, cloud providers, and networks rent space and connect directly to each other. Great businesses own scarce, irreplaceable locations; average ones rent commodity space.

4. The 4 Macro Drivers

Driver 1: Long-Term Interest Rates

Mechanism: REITs are long-duration bonds with growth. Higher 10-year yields lift the discount rate on decades of future rent → multiples compress → these names de-rate. Debt also reprices on refinancing.
Now: The 6-month lag vs SPY (-15.42pts) is the rate scar; the recent tower bounce signals yields easing. Rate relief rotates money back to the most bond-like names (towers).
2nd-order effect: Juniors watch the de-rate; the money is in the refi wall. Falling rates cut future interest expense, lifting FFO per share two years out.
Threshold: 10-year decisively below 4% re-rates the whole group.

Driver 2: AI/Hyperscale Capex Cycle

Mechanism: Cloud and AI buildouts create demand for colocation and hyperscale space → leasing accelerates → rents and pre-leasing rates rise.
Now: Demand is overwhelming supply. Equinix's CEO said demand is clearly exceeding the assumptions in the prior long-term outlook.

Digital Realty signed a 200-MW AI inference lease, its largest hyperscale deal ever.

2nd-order effect: Everyone sees leasing; few model the capex drag. Massive builds mean equity issuance and dilution before the cash flow arrives.
Threshold: Hyperscaler capex guidance cuts = first crack in the thesis.

Driver 3: Power Availability

Mechanism: A data center without power is empty real estate. Grid interconnection queues, not land, now cap new supply → scarcity value accrues to owners of powered capacity.
Now: Power is the bottleneck. Equinix controls roughly 3 GW of designed power, with about 600 MW under development and an expected 1 GW under production by early next year.

A planned $100B energy hub ties Brookfield and NextEra to long-duration power and compute demand few investors are modeling.

2nd-order effect: Scarcity lets incumbents raise rents on existing powered halls — pricing power without spending.
Threshold: Grid interconnection timelines shortening nationally.

Driver 4: Tenant Concentration & Carrier Capex (Towers)

Mechanism: Towers lease to 3-4 carriers. Carrier capex cycles and merger-driven churn swing tower revenue directly → consolidation means fewer tenants, lower co-location growth.
Now: Towers rebounded on rate relief and oversold value, not fundamentals — American Tower leads peers with the highest yield near 4.44%, supported by data center revenue growth.

2nd-order effect: Juniors cheer 5G densification; the real swing is Sprint/DISH-style churn hitting organic growth two years later.
Threshold: Carrier capex guidance up + churn normalizing = durable tower leg.

7. How to Value These Companies

Use FFO/AFFO multiples and dividend yield, never P/E — depreciation on long-lived buildings is a huge non-cash charge that makes EPS meaningless here. For development-heavy names, add stabilized yield-on-cost vs cap rate (the development spread is where value is created). Typical AFFO multiples run 18-25x; towers trade on yield. The classic junior mistake: anchoring on headline dividend yield while ignoring the payout ratio and dilution from equity issuance funding growth.

9. Risk Map

Risk 1: Rate-Driven Multiple Compression

Long-duration cash flows get discounted harder as yields rise → multiples compress and refinancing costs climb → FFO per share falls. The 2022-23 selloff saw tower and data center REITs halve on a 500bp rate move. The 6-month lag vs SPY (-15.42pts) is the live scar. Early warning: 10-year yield breaking higher and credit spreads widening on REIT paper.

Risk 2: Hyperscaler Self-Build / Bypass

The biggest tenants build their own campuses and buy power directly, cutting out the landlord → leasing slows and renewal pricing weakens → growth multiple collapses. This is the structural fear overhanging DLR's largest deals. Early warning: hyperscaler capex shifting toward owned facilities, declining third-party lease announcements, and pre-leasing percentages drifting down.

Risk 3: Overbuild / Oversupply

Easy capital funds a building boom → too much capacity in hot markets → occupancy and rents fall, yields on cost compress. Classic late-cycle REIT trap; Northern Virginia has flirted with this repeatedly. With $20B pipelines industry-wide, the risk is real. Early warning: stabilized yields falling below ~9%, rising vacancy, speculative builds without pre-leases.

Risk 4: Tenant Concentration & Carrier Consolidation

Three carriers dominate tower revenue; one merger removes a tenant → churn spikes, organic growth stalls for years. The Sprint/T-Mobile merger drained tower organic growth well after closing. AMT and CCI still carry this scar. Early warning: merger announcements among major carriers, rising churn guidance, and co-location additions slowing.

11. Structural Themes

Theme 1: Power as the New Scarcity

Compute demand has outrun the grid; the bottleneck shifted from land to megawatts. AI's energy intensity is accelerating this now. A planned $100B energy hub ties power and compute demand together in ways few investors model. Winners: owners of secured, powered capacity who can raise rents on scarcity. Losers: developers stuck in interconnection queues. Position before consensus by favoring names disclosing contracted power pipelines, not just land banks.

Theme 2: AI Inference Moves to the Edge

Training clustered in mega-campuses; inference needs low-latency sites near users — exactly where interconnection-dense colo lives. This accelerates as AI apps go mainstream. Digital Realty's 200-MW AI inference lease was its largest hyperscale deal ever. Winners: Equinix-style interconnection hubs. Losers: remote single-purpose halls. Position toward edge/interconnection density before the market fully separates it from commodity hyperscale.

13. Three Questions You Should Be Able to Answer

Q1: Why do data center REITs issue so much equity if demand is booming?
A: Because booming demand requires booming capex before cash arrives. A $20B pipeline takes years to stabilize, but builds need funding today. REIT rules force ~90% payout, leaving little retained cash, so they tap equity and debt. DLR sold 7.3M ATM shares for ~$1.3B and raised capex guidance to $4.25-4.75B. The subtlety: dilution can erode per-share growth even as total revenue soars — watch share count, not just headlines.

Q2: Why did towers soar while data centers fell this month despite AI tailwinds?
A: Rotation, not fundamentals. Towers are the most bond-like, rate-sensitive names; they cratered over six months on rate fear ( AMT near its 52-week low at $162.20 ). As yields eased, capital rotated from expensive, perfectly-priced data centers into oversold towers. The transmission: rate relief → duration re-rates → the cheapest duration proxy bounces hardest. CCI +12.6%, EQIX -3.5% in the same month tells the whole story.

Q3: Bull vs bear on the group given today's macro?
A: Bull: AI demand is structural and exceeding every forecast; power scarcity hands incumbents pricing power; rate relief re-rates the whole group. Bear: valuations are stretched ( EQIX trailing P/E 64.78 ), hyperscaler self-build threatens the biggest deals, and overbuild looms. What flips it: the 10-year yield's direction and whether October earnings show leasing still accelerating. Rates down + backlog up = bull wins.
